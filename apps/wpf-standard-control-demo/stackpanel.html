StackPanel Layout

StackPanel places its children one after another in a single row or column. It is the simplest panel, and the one whose sizing most often surprises.

Overview

A StackPanel gives each child unlimited space in the stacking direction. In a vertical panel 200 × 100, a child was measured with 200 × Infinity; in a horizontal one, with Infinity × 100. In the other direction the child is stretched: a child that wanted 40 × 20 was arranged 200 wide in the vertical panel and 100 high in the horizontal one. Because of the unlimited space, a list in a vertical StackPanel does not get a limited height. A ListBox of 1000 items in an area 200 high was 200 high, could scroll by 991, and created 10 items. Put inside a vertical StackPanel in the same area, it grew to 19964 high, could not scroll, and created all 1000 items. The article linked below shows the same for a ScrollViewer.

A StackPanel does not virtualize. With 1000 children in a StackPanel inside a ScrollViewer 200 high, all 1000 were measured. There is also no Spacing property; space children with their Margin.

The demo app has three sections: Orientation, Background, and ZIndex. The "Show Code" link under each section displays its XAML.

Demonstrated Properties

The following properties are demonstrated interactively in the WPF Standard Control Demo App. The behavior described for each one was measured on .NET 10 (see Measured Behavior).

Property Values Description
Orientation Horizontal / Vertical The stacking direction; the default is Vertical. Children never wrap. With the demo app's three labels in a horizontal StackPanel 80 wide, the third label started at 94.69, past the panel's edge. The part beyond the edge is cut off: ClipToBounds was False, but WPF's layout clip limited the panel to its 80, and a hit test on the third label beyond that edge found nothing. Use WrapPanel to wrap.
Background (Panel) Brush The fill of the panel, and whether its empty area can be clicked. The demo app shows an empty StackPanel 30 high. With the default null, a hit test in its middle found nothing, so clicks there go to what is behind it. With Transparent or a color, the same hit test found the panel.
ZIndex (Panel attached) int Which child is drawn on top where children overlap. In the demo app, the second label overlaps the first by 15. With ZIndex 1 and 2, the second label was on top at the overlap; with 3 and 2, the first. It only orders children of the same panel. A child of an inner panel with ZIndex="100" stayed under a sibling of that inner panel whose ZIndex was 0.

XAML Example

The following XAML is the ZIndex section of the demo app (StackPanelUsageControl.xaml), with the styles and the surrounding GroupBoxes left out:

<TextBox x:Name="ZIndexItem1Text" Text="1" />
<TextBox x:Name="ZIndexItem2Text" Text="2" />

<StackPanel x:Name="ZIndexStackPanel">
  <Label VerticalAlignment="Top"
         Panel.ZIndex="{Binding Text, ElementName=ZIndexItem1Text}"
         Background="LightBlue" BorderBrush="Black" BorderThickness="1"
         Content="Item1" />
  <Label Margin="0,-15,0,0" VerticalAlignment="Top"
         Panel.ZIndex="{Binding Text, ElementName=ZIndexItem2Text}"
         Background="SkyBlue" BorderBrush="Black" BorderThickness="1"
         Content="Item2" />
</StackPanel>

Common Use Cases

  • Button rows: OK and Cancel side by side in a horizontal StackPanel.
  • Short forms: a label and a field under each other in a vertical StackPanel.
  • Content of a ScrollViewer: a column of sections that the ScrollViewer scrolls as a whole.

Tips and Best Practices

  • Do not put a ListBox or ScrollViewer in a vertical StackPanel if it should scroll. Give it a limited height, for example a Grid row.
  • Do not use a StackPanel for long lists. It measures every child. A ListBox with a limited height created only the items it showed.
  • Set Background="Transparent" for a clickable empty area. The default null lets clicks through.
  • Use ZIndex within one panel. It cannot lift a child above elements outside its own panel.

Measured Behavior

Every behavior on this page was measured by running it on .NET 10 / Windows 11, using StackPanelDemoScene in this site's screenshot tool. The panels were laid out with Measure and Arrange, and the element on top was found by a hit test at the given point. Sizes are the values on the measuring machine.
